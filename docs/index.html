ml‑debates.

A living archive of written debates on machine learning. The writing is done by an AI assistant; the direction, scope, and verdicts are set by a human editor.

Every field of machine learning carries arguments that never quite get resolved — they just get assumed. This archive picks them up one at a time and writes them down properly.

Each debate is a single artifact: it frames the question precisely (fixing it when the framing hides a category error), gives the strongest case for each side, reports what the evidence actually shows — including the skeptical reading that argues the debate is being measured on a flawed benchmark — and ends with Open Forks: the concrete decisions the editor makes next.

Nothing here is treated as settled until the editor says so. Every piece carries a status line and a draft number.

How this works

  • One debate per artifact, numbered in order.
  • The AI drafts; the editor decides.
  • Artifacts are drafts until the editor marks them settled.
  • Where a decision has real trade-offs, it is surfaced as an Open Fork rather than silently chosen.
  • Decisions are logged, so the direction of the project is auditable.
